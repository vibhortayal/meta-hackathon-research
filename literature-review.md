# Literature review: AI harms and wishes, household coordination, personal and local-first agents

2026-10-26. Every entry in the main tables was opened on its primary page (arXiv abstract, publisher or repository record, agency release or report PDF) during this pass, unless marked "abstract only". "Opened" means I read what the page showed; several publisher pages blocked access, so those papers are in the unverified list at the end, not in the tables. Evidence ratings are my judgement of how far the source supports a claim about people in general: **strong** (large, representative or official data, or a well-designed study), **moderate** (sound but narrow, self-report, single-site, or benchmark-only), **weak** (preprint, tiny sample, position paper, or tangential), **anecdotal**.

## B. Time, mental load, household coordination, notification fatigue

| # | Source | Method and sample | Main finding | Strength and why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | [Daminger, dissertation 2022](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0594bb9c-1c9d-4f31-bcde-00ad47bf3d7c/content) (the ASR 2019 article is not opened, see below) | Interviews, 76 different-sex couples, 136 interviews | Defines cognitive labor as anticipating needs, identifying options, deciding, and monitoring results. Equality is more common in physical than cognitive labor | Moderate: rich qualitative work, not representative, different-sex couples only |
| B2 | [BLS American Time Use Survey, 2025 data](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm) | National time diary | On days they did household activities, women averaged 2.8 hours and men 2.1; 66% did food prep and cleanup on weekdays; employed parents of under-6s have 3.2 hours of leisure vs 4.5 for employed adults with no young children. Data gap from the autumn 2025 shutdown | Strong for time, but it measures time, not mental load |
| B3 | [Mehrotra et al., CHI 2016](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/my-phone-and-me-understanding-peoples-receptivity-to-mobile-notif/) | In-the-wild logging, 10,372 notifications, 474 responses, 20 users | Even useful notifications disrupt; disruption depends on task, sender relationship and personality | Moderate: peer-reviewed, tiny user sample |
| B4 | [Amagai et al., JMIR 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35471414) | Literature review, 62 mobile-health studies | Participants were more likely to drop out than stay; well-timed reminders, feedback and support helped retention; low perceived usefulness hurt | Moderate: health apps and research settings, not household tools |
| B5 | [Kazakoff and Doyle-Baker 2026](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42676439) (abstract) | 293 app users over 90 days | Low-engagement users' perceived ease of use fell significantly; high-engagement users kept theirs | Weak: one app category, abstract only |
| B6 | [Koh et al., JMIR 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41447266) | Scoping review of 35 weight-management JITAI studies | Prompts were the most common support; 31% used machine learning, rest rules; more engagement went with more weight loss; reporting was inconsistent | Moderate: review, narrow domain |
| B7 | [Nahum-Shani et al., dyadic JITAIs, 2026](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/xsu45_v1) (PsyArXiv preprint) | Conceptual design paper | Argues just-in-time interventions should involve close partners, not only individuals, and needs new theory and trials | Weak: preprint, no data. Relevant as the nearest "couple-level nudging" framing I found |
| B8 | [Chang et al., PARTNR (Meta FAIR)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00081), 2024 | 100,000 simulated household tasks, 60 houses | LLMs coordinate poorly and recover poorly from errors; with real humans they need about 1.5x the steps of two humans | Moderate: simulation benchmark, embodied tasks, not scheduling |

## E. What the evidence supports for the project

- **Time and life admin is what people say they want.** Life management plus time freedom are 24.6% of primary hopes in the 81k interviews (A6), but the sample is Claude users. Pew (A7) shows broad nervousness, not wishes.
- **Privacy worry is large and justified.** 71% expect less security of personal information (A7); every frontier developer reviewed trains on chats by default (A14). This supports a local-first position, as a concern, not proof people will pay for it.
- **Reliability is the top fear (26.7%, A6) and agents are inconsistent (C3; C2 gets about two-thirds right on when to help).** A household agent that nudges wrongly will lose trust; "abstain or ask" and a nudge budget are justified.
- **Mental load is real, anticipating and monitoring are its heaviest parts (B1).** That matches the "remembering" gap Vibhor described. The evidence is interview-based and about different-sex couples.
- **Notification evidence is thin for our case.** Useful notifications still disrupt (B3) and well-timed reminders help retention in health apps (B4), but I found nothing about household tools or LLM nudges. Dyadic nudging (B7) is only a preprint.
- **Deskilling is plausible but the strong evidence is clinical (A4), observational; the broad "AI hurts thinking" claims are self-report or preprint (A2, A3).** Do not pitch deskilling as proven.
- **Anecdotal only:** the "I want to cook with my mother" type quotes and HN comments; the vendor planner-abandonment statistics.
- **Gaps:** no peer-reviewed study I could open on why shared family calendars die; no evidence on a vision model reading a whiteboard (C7 is page handwriting, no Spark or Glimmer); nothing on small local models doing reliable tool calls for personal tasks (C4 is opinion); no AI-specific scam figures from FBI or FTC. Spikes should fill the whiteboard and local-tool-calling gaps.
