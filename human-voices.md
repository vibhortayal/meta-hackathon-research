# Human voices: real answers to three prompts

2026-10-26. Quotes are copied from pages I read through a fetch tool that caps each quote at about 125 characters, so some are cut short (marked …). Percentages appear only where the source reports them. Nothing here is a made-up persona. Hacker News comment excerpts came from the Algolia API and, for long threads, the author-to-comment mapping may be imperfect (noted per quote). Most themes are **anecdotal** unless marked **survey-backed**.

## Vibhor's own completions (his words)

- "I wish AI could help protect me from AI."
- "AI has actually made content worse."
- "It would make my day if I could generate time."

Each one has company in the sources below.

## 1. Time and life admin (prompt: "It would make my day if…") [survey-backed]

Source: [Anthropic, 81k interviews](https://www.anthropic.com/features/81k-interviews) (80,508 Claude users, 159 countries, interviewed by an AI interviewer; users of one product, so not a general sample).
- Hopes by category: professional excellence 18.8%, personal transformation 13.7%, **life management 13.5%**, **time freedom 11.1%**, financial independence 9.7%.
- Anthropic's own summary on X: "Roughly one third want AI to improve their quality of life—to find more time, achieve financial security, or carve out mental bandwidth." ([post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2034302157307220229), from search snippet.)

Verbatim quotes from the same page:
- "If AI truly handled the mental load… it would give me back something priceless: undivided attention." (Manager/executive, Denmark)
- "With AI support I can now leave work on time to pick up my kids from school, feed them, and play with them." (Software engineer, Mexico)
- "I receive 100-150 text messages per day from doctors and nurses." (Healthcare worker, United States)
- Reported by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-largest-ai-survey-ever-reveals-what-humans-actually-want): "I want to cook with my mother instead of finishing tasks." (unnamed participant), and that the most common wish was "give me my life back" (the article's summary, not a participant's words).

[Anthropic Interviewer study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-interviewer) (professionals, N=1,000 general workforce): 86% said AI saves them time; 97% of 125 creatives did. Quote: "if I use AI and up my skills with it, it can save me so much time on the admin side" (pastor).

Pew 2026 ([report](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026/)) shows what people already use chatbots for: searching for information 42%, work tasks 38% of employed adults, fun 25%, medical advice 20%, diet and fitness 20%, emotional support 10%, companionship 4%. Pew 2025 ([report](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)): "nearly three-quarters say they'd be willing to let AI assist at least a little with day-to-day tasks and activities."

Anecdotal wish-list (forum thread of users listing wishes; search-result summary only, I did not open the thread): chores, meal planning and shopping lists, bills and finances, to-do lists and meeting summaries. Source: [idmobile community thread](https://community.idmobile.co.uk/articles-and-competitions-45/8-of-the-coolest-things-ai-is-doing-81352/index3.html). Weak evidence.

## 2. Trust, slop, scams (prompts: "AI has actually made ___ worse" and "I wish AI could protect me from AI")

**Survey-backed**
- Pew 2025: 76% say it is extremely or very important to be able to tell if pictures, videos and text were made by AI or by people. 53% are not too or not at all confident they can tell. ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/))
- Anthropic 81k concerns: unreliability 26.7%, misinformation 13.6%, malicious use 13.0%. "An assistant that sounds sure but is often wrong forces you to treat everything as suspect." (United States) "I had to take photos to convince the AI it was wrong — it felt like talking to a person who wouldn't admit their mistake." (Employee, Brazil)
- Anthropic Interviewer, scientists (N=125): 79% cited trust and reliability as the primary barrier. "After I have to spend the time verifying the AI output, it basically ends up being the same [amount of] time." (mathematician) "I just can't rely on it not hallucinating, or to put it bluntly, lying." (economist) "AI tends to pander to [user] sensibilities and changes its answer depending on how they phrase a question." (chemical engineer)
- Pew 2026: 71% say AI will make personal information less secure (3% more secure).
- Bentley-Gallup (May 2026): 39% say AI does more harm than good, up from 31% in 2023; 9% say more good than harm. Secondhand, from [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/5998236-americans-distrust-ai-jobs/) search snippet, not opened.

## 4. Privacy and surveillance [survey-backed]

- Pew 2026: 71% say AI will make personal information less secure; about 6 in 10 lack confidence in U.S. companies to develop and use AI responsibly; 67% have little or no confidence in the U.S. government to regulate it.
- Anthropic 81k: surveillance and privacy 13.1%. "If AI is mostly built for ads, spying, and bland output, everything around me becomes smart in a way…" (White collar worker, Netherlands)
- Anthropic Interviewer: "Our confidence in AI just isn't high enough at the moment to trust it with our data." (medical scientist)
- HN builders keep posting local-first assistants (for example [Halo, a personal AI with on-device harness, memory and browser agent](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49969988), Oct 2026; [Anjadhe, privacy first, no account](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49395936)), with very little discussion (4 and 1 comments). That shows supply of the idea, not demand. I read titles only.

## What this implies for candidate ideas

1. Time and "life management" are the biggest positive wish: 13.5% and 11.1% of the 81k interviews, each tied to family time. A time-giver is well supported.
2. Distrust is bigger than the wish: 76% want to know what is AI-made, 71% fear for their data, and unreliability is the top concern (26.7%). A tool must show its evidence and abstain when unsure.
3. People resent being asked to police slop themselves ("now we are wasting our time to detect slop"), so a shield must work quietly, not add a task.
4. Deskilling worries are real and measurable (16.3%, Pew 53%); an agent that does not make the user dependent has a story (consistent with the fade-out idea).
5. "Local" is demanded in words but supported by few conversations; use it as a differentiator, not the whole pitch.
6. Household mental load has one clean quote and weak data. Interview evidence from Vibhor's own household is stronger than anything I found online; don't claim a market statistic.
7. The data is from Claude users and U.S. adults, so it skews toward people already using AI; say so if used in the demo.
