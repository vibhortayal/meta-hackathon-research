# Unmet needs landscape: where the evidence shows a gap AI could fill

2026-10-26. A broad evidence pass, not limited to AI harms or household load (the sibling `literature-review.md` covers AI harms, mental load, planner abandonment and local-first agents; this file does not repeat it). Method: I opened primary pages where I could and marked the rest as read-through-search-summary. Anything I could not open is in "Unverified". Strength ratings are mine: **strong** = large, well-run survey, government statistic or randomized/meta-analytic evidence; **moderate** = decent data with a caveat (self-selected sample, secondary coverage, advocacy analysis, or old); **weak** = small sample, vendor or aggregator figure, or a claim I could not trace.

## How to read the "what a solo build could do" lines

Constraints I used: one builder, about 6 days, Meta models (Spark vision, video and tool calling; Voice Transcribe realtime; SAM 3.1; Glimmer local), phone camera and headphones, Hindi and English, optional local-first, and the hackathon avoid list (AI medical or mental-health advice, basic RAG, simple image analyzers, education chatbots, job screeners, nutrition coaches, personality analyzers, sports analyzers, political or war content). Meta has no text-to-speech, so spoken output uses the browser's speech synthesis.

The stated fear that matters most for design is unreliability (26.7%) followed by autonomy (21.9%), which argues for agents that abstain and ask rather than act silently.

## 2. Domain by domain

### Time poverty (cross-cutting)
- **Gap:** most people feel persistently short of time; in one analysis of 2.5 million Americans, feeling time-poor hurt wellbeing more than being unemployed. [Giurge, Whillans and West, Nature Human Behaviour 2020](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/1451) (abstract and secondary summary only; the "80% never have enough time" figure traces to a different source and I could not verify it). Moderate.
- **Hard data:** US adults spend about 2 hours a day on household activities (81% do some); women 2.8 hours on days they do, men 2.1; primary childcare with a child under 6 is 2.3 hours a day, women 2.8 and men 1.7. [BLS American Time Use Survey 2025, released June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm) (opened). Strong.
- **AI already serving it:** generic assistants (Instinct, Zazu, Housemait, see the household doc). **Solo build:** one narrow time-giver such as the household agent (see `household-agent-candidate.md`).

### Caregiving, eldercare, sandwich generation
- **Gap:** 63 million US family caregivers (nearly 1 in 4 adults), up 20 million since 2015; they average 27 hours a week, nearly 1 in 4 give 40 or more; 6 in 10 are employed and half of those report work disruptions; nearly half report a major financial hit; about $7,200 a year out of pocket; only 11% had medical training for their tasks although over half handle complex tasks like injections, wound care or medication management; nearly 1 in 4 feel socially isolated; 1 in 5 are in fair or poor health. [AARP and National Alliance for Caregiving, Caregiving in the US 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html) (opened). Strong. The full report's coordination findings were not retrievable.
- **AI already serving it:** mostly health Q&A (Copilot, ChatGPT). I did not find a widely used caregiver handoff or paperwork agent. **Unverified** that none exists.
- **Solo build:** a non-medical "care ledger": photograph a discharge sheet, pill bottle or appointment card, get a structured schedule, a shared handoff note for siblings, and reminders. Must not give medical advice.

### Health and wellbeing (self-management, adherence, sleep, activity, loneliness)
- **Chronic disease:** about 90% of the nation's $5.3 trillion health spending goes to people with chronic and mental health conditions; physical inactivity costs $192 billion; diabetes $640 billion. [CDC, chronic disease facts](https://www.cdc.gov/chronic-disease/data-research/facts-stats/index.html) (opened). Strong.
- **Medication adherence:** WHO's widely cited claim is that adherence to long-term therapy averages 50% in developed countries (hypertension 51% in the US). A critique found the 50% figure rests on two old studies (1965 and 1975), so treat it as a rough benchmark, not a measured rate. [WHO 2003 release](https://www.who.int/news/item/01-07-2003-failure-to-take-prescribed-medicine-for-chronic-diseases-is-a-massive-world-wide-problem); [critique, PMC3525884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3525884). Weak to moderate. I could not open the CDC adherence page (404).
- **Sleep and activity:** CDC reports state-level insufficient sleep from 30% (Vermont) to 46% (Hawaii) in 2022, with the national share flat since 2013; the page gives no national figure. The adult guideline is 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus strength work twice weekly, but the page gives no share meeting it. [CDC sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/data-research/facts-stats/adults-sleep-facts-and-stats.html), [CDC activity guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) (both opened). Strong, but incomplete.
- **Loneliness:** the 2023 Surgeon General advisory says about half of US adults report loneliness and compares the mortality effect of disconnection to up to 15 cigarettes a day. I could not open the primary PDF (403); figures come from press coverage of it. [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1173418268). Moderate. Chatbots are not a clean fix: an MIT Media Lab randomized trial (981 participants, 4 weeks) found heavier daily use correlated with more loneliness, dependence and less socializing, which the authors caution is correlation. [Fang et al., arXiv 2503.17473](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17473) (via search summary). Moderate.
- **Rule note:** medical and mental-health advice is on the avoid list. **Solo build:** logistics around health (appointments, refill reminders, shared schedules), not advice. A "keep promises to your own routine" nudger is possible but sits near the coach category.

### Work: admin load, meetings, small businesses
- **Gap:** small-business owners report large admin loads, but the numbers conflict: 36% of the work week (Censuswide, 251 US entrepreneurs, 2023), 23 hours a week (SCORE, unverified), 11 hours (Amex UK, 1,000 owners, 2026), 22.4% of owner time for microbusinesses (UENI, 837 firms, 2024). [Search-summary coverage](https://retailtimes.co.uk/amex-sme-barometer-small-business-owners-spend-nearly-twice-the-time-on-admin-than-growing-their-business/). Weak, because definitions differ.
- **Microsoft Work Trend Index** is the obvious source for meeting overload; its page listed reports but no figures, so I have no verified number. [Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index) (opened, no stats). **Unverified.**
- **AI already serving it:** QuickBooks, Expensify and others for receipts and invoices (crowded). **Solo build:** receipt and invoice photo plus voice to a ledger for a tiny shop; competes with funded tools, so it needs a narrow user (for example a street vendor or home baker).

### Learning and language
- **Tutoring:** the Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan meta-analysis found tutoring raises learning, pooled effect 0.37 SD in the 2020 paper and 0.288 SD in the 2024 published version (89 studies), largest with teachers or paraprofessionals, 3 or more days a week, during school. [NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476) (via search summary). Strong. The gap is that human tutoring does not scale, but "simple AI-for-education chatbots" are on the avoid list, so a plain tutor is out.
- **Adult literacy:** about one-third of US adults (roughly 59 to 60 million) read at or below PIAAC Level 1. [ProLiteracy summary of PIAAC](https://proliteracy.org/news/page/3) (via search summary; OECD primary: [adults with low proficiency](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/adults-with-low-proficiency-in-literacy-or-numeracy_5jm0v44bnmnx-en.html)). Moderate. Dyslexia prevalence: I could not find a verified adult population figure.
- **Language barriers:** 25 to 29.6 million US residents have limited English proficiency (the two figures use different definitions); a 2024 scoping review of 137 studies found poorer access and outcomes for them. [Scoping review, PMC10855368](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10855368/) (via search summary). Moderate. I did not find the Migration Policy Institute figure.
- **Solo build:** a Hindi and English two-way helper for appointments and paperwork (live speech in via Voice Transcribe, Spark translation, browser speech out). Latency is unmeasured.

### Money, benefits and scams
- **Scams:** consumers reported $15.9 billion in fraud losses in 2025 (3 million reports), up from over $12 billion in 2024; investment scams were the biggest loss at over $7.9 billion; imposter scams the most reported with over $3.5 billion; bank transfers then crypto carried the most loss; the FTC says true cost may be far higher (one estimate $195.9 billion for 2024). [FTC testimony to the Joint Economic Committee, March 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-testimony-jec-hearing-on-the-rising-scam-economy.pdf) (opened, figures confirmed from the text). Strong. People 60 and over reported about $3 billion in 2025, up from about $600 million in 2020 ([FTC older-adults report, via search summary](https://www.ftc.gov/node/338049); moderate). In India, reported cyber-fraud cases were about 2.81 million in 2025 and the cybercrime coordination centre projects up to ₹1.2 lakh crore in losses, though the voice-cloning counts I saw come from aggregators and one regional police claim I could not confirm. [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/fraud-alert-india-is-losing-over-22000-crore-a-year-in-cyber-scams-and-the-worst-is-yet-to-come/80612.html). Weak to moderate.
- **AI already serving it:** [Norton Genie](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6448706515) checks pasted or screenshotted texts, emails and sites; a tester got "not sure" on an obvious scam text (single review). I found nothing that screens a live call or coordinates a family response.
- **Benefits take-up:** USDA estimates 82% of eligible people received SNAP in FY2019; Urban Institute analysis found only about 30% of eligible adults 65 and over were enrolled, with almost 9 million eligible but not enrolled. [USDA FNS](https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/snap/state-participation-rates/2019), [Urban Institute via search summary](https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/rsLYY/5/). Moderate. Eligibility modeling varies by method.
- **Financial capability:** FINRA's national study found in 2015 that 34% could not come up with $2,000 in 30 days (45% among those with a high school education or less). Old, and I could not find the 2024 wave. [FINRA Foundation](https://syndication.finra.org/node/206). Moderate but dated.
- **Solo build:** a scam checker only clears the avoid list's "simple image analyzer" bar if it acts: a call-back verification flow, a family safe-word check, an alert to a trusted contact, and an optional local-only mode.

### Community, civic life and amateur leagues
- **Volunteering:** 75.7 million people (28.3% of those 16 and over) formally volunteered between Sept 2022 and Sept 2023, up 5.1 points from 2022, though hours per volunteer keep falling. [AmeriCorps and Census, Volunteering and Civic Life 2023](https://americorps.gov/newsroom/press-release/more-757-million-people-volunteered-america-americorps-reports) (via search summary). Strong.
- **Youth sports:** costs are up 46% since 2019, 55.4% of children 6 to 17 played sports in 2023, and most of the roughly 6.5 million youth coaches are volunteers (that last figure is from 2018). [Aspen Project Play, State of Play 2025](https://projectplay.org/state-of-play-2025) (via search summary). Moderate.
- **Tournament and league software:** crowded on scheduling (PickleCourt, Round Robin Assistant, PlayMore, PlayPBNow, and others). [Search summary](https://www.playpickleball.com/playmore-app-pickleball-scheduling-round-robin/). The messy layer (organizers fielding schedule changes, no-shows and substitutions across WhatsApp) was not covered by what I found. **Unverified** gap. Also close to the avoid-list "sports analyzer" only if it analyzes play; scheduling is fine.
- **Solo build:** an organizer's agent that reads a group chat, applies changes and keeps the bracket current. It ties directly to Vibhor's tournament app.

### Accessibility
- **Gap:** over 2.5 billion people need at least one assistive product, rising to 3.5 billion by 2050; in some low-income countries as few as 3% of those who need them can get them; hearing aid production meets under 10% of demand; 2.2 billion people have a vision impairment, at least 1 billion preventable or unaddressed. [WHO assistive technology](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/assistive-technology), [WHO vision](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/blindness-and-visual-impairment) (both opened). Strong.
- **AI already serving it:** Be My AI (over 900,000 blind and low-vision users per Wikipedia, a weak source) and Seeing AI. Studies of 14 users found real value from rich descriptions plus recurring hallucinated context and misread intent, and a follow-up argues users want more control over detail and efficiency. [arXiv 2407.08882](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08882), [arXiv 2502.16098](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16098v1), [arXiv 2602.16930](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16930v1) (via search summaries; n=14). Moderate to weak. In Anthropic's survey 8.7% said AI had already enabled technical accessibility, and people called it "disability infrastructure".
- **Solo build:** an "ask the room" helper with calibrated uncertainty and user-set verbosity, in Hindi and English. It risks the "simple image analyzer" label unless it acts (find, track and guide to an object using SAM 3.1) and abstains visibly.

### Aging in place and safety
- **Gap:** 75% of Americans 50 and over want to stay in their current home as long as possible (AARP 2024, n=3,090); fewer than 4% of single-family homes can be easily modified for mobility needs; 1 in 4 adults 65 and over fall each year (over 14 million), and the age-adjusted fall death rate rose 21% from 2018 to 2024. [AARP home and community preferences](https://datastories.aarp.org/2025/building-for-the-future) (via search summary), [CDC falls](https://www.cdc.gov/falls/data-research/index.html) (opened). Strong.
- **Solo build:** home cameras plus elder monitoring raise consent and medical-claim issues. Local Glimmer helps privacy but this is the riskiest fit for a 6-day build.

### Climate and home energy
- **Gap:** surveys point to trust and effort in choosing contractors, not only cost, as barriers; households cite climate as a motivation far more than contractors think (89% versus 44%). [PNNL 2025](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/decarbonizing-residential-buildings-united-states-comparative-analysis-households-and), [Michigan Graham Institute](https://graham.umich.edu/media/files/Home-Energy-Retrofits-Adoption-Pathways.pdf), [ESRI Ireland](https://esri.ie/publications/residential-energy-retrofits-how-disruption-impacts-homeowner-decisions) (all via search summaries). Moderate.
- Already assessed in `research.md` as the energy retrofit fallback. I found no DOE or IEA 2025 backlog figure.

### Immigration and bureaucracy
- **Gap:** pending USCIS cases reached about 11.3 million in FY2026 Q1 and 12.1 million in Q2 despite a 45% drop in received forms; EB-2 national interest waiver denial rates rose from 37.3% to 57.4%. [American Immigration Council](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/blog/uscis-backlog-grows-less-immigration-filings/) (via search summary, advocacy analysis of USCIS data). Moderate. Vibhor has dropped this direction; see `immigration-candidate.md`.

### Creativity
- **Gap:** creative expression is the primary wish of 5.6% in Anthropic's survey and 11.7% fear loss of meaning and creativity; Pew finds more than half expect AI to hurt creative thinking (secondary coverage). I found no study quantifying an unmet creative need beyond these. Weak. **Solo build:** outside this brief's strongest evidence; not ranked.

### Things the evidence surfaced that nobody named
- **"Illusory productivity":** 18% of respondents in Anthropic's survey said work speeds up but rest does not grow. A product that returns saved time to the user (blocks, protects or hands it back) is an under-served angle. Moderate.
- **Health questions about someone else:** secondary coverage of the Copilot report says roughly 1 in 7 personal health queries concern another person (child, parent, partner). That supports a caregiver-support product, but I could not verify it in the primary report. Weak.
- **Weekend and evening spikes:** recipes at 6 PM and sleep questions at 5 AM show demand clustered in specific household moments, which suits a nudge-at-the-right-moment design. Moderate.
